The short version

  • Gap between spending and pensions, divided by a safe payout rate.
  • Example: 24,000 a year gap at 3.5% is about 686,000.
  • A simulation is more reliable than a rule of thumb.

Start with the yearly gap: what you spend minus the pensions you will receive. Divide the gap by a safe withdrawal rate such as 3.5% to get a first estimate of the pot you need. It is a rule of thumb. A planner that simulates many possible futures is more reliable, because it also looks at returns, inflation and tax.

The quick estimate

  1. Write down your yearly spending in today's euros.
  2. Subtract the pensions you will receive each year (Dutch State Pension, workplace pension, Indian pension).
  3. Divide the gap by 0.035 (3.5%).

Example. You want EUR 36,000 a year. Pensions give EUR 12,000 a year. The gap is EUR 24,000. The first estimate is 24,000 / 0.035 = about EUR 686,000.

Why a rule of thumb is not enough

  • Pensions start at different ages, so the gap changes over time.
  • Returns are uneven. A bad first five years hurts more than a bad last five.
  • Tax differs between the Netherlands and India, and Box 3 taxes the pot every year.
  • Where you live changes your monthly cost.

How the planner defines "needed"

The planner shows the smallest pot that lasts to your chosen age if returns come in at the average. It then also shows how often your actual pot survives across many random futures. If you choose to leave an inheritance, it looks for a pot that still holds that amount at the end, and the safe payout rate (3.5% by default, changeable under Assumptions) applies to that case.

Rules of thumb. The 3.5% rate is the planner default, not a guarantee. Lower it for a longer retirement or a more cautious view.

Frequently asked

What is a safe withdrawal rate?

The share of your pot you take out in the first year, adjusted for inflation afterwards. 3.5% is the planner default. You can change it.

How much do I need to retire in India?

It depends on your spending in India, your pensions and your tax status. Enter your own numbers in the planner.
