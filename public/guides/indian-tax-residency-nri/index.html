NRI, RNOR or resident? Indian tax status if you split your year

The short version

  • India counts days in its financial year (1 April to 31 March).
  • NRI, RNOR and resident are taxed differently.
  • Splitting the year puts you near the line.

India decides your tax status by counting days in the Indian financial year (1 April to 31 March). Under 182 days usually keeps you a non-resident (NRI). For some Indian citizens with higher Indian income there is a stricter test. A status in between, RNOR, is a temporary middle level. Your status decides which of your income India taxes.

Three statuses

StatusWhat India taxes
NRI (non-resident)Income earned or received in India
RNOR (resident but not ordinarily resident)Income earned or received in India (plus some income controlled from India)
Resident and ordinarily residentYour income from anywhere in the world

The day counts (secondary sources)

  • You are generally a resident if you are in India 182 days or more in the financial year, or a shorter stay plus a long stay in the previous four years.
  • For an Indian citizen or person of Indian origin with Indian income above 15 lakh a year, the stricter threshold is 120 days, and the person then becomes resident but not ordinarily resident.
  • A returning person is RNOR if they were an NRI in 9 of the previous 10 years, or spent 729 days or fewer in India in the previous 7 years.

Why this matters if you split your year

If you plan to spend 6 months in India and 6 months in the Netherlands, you sit right at the line. The planner warns you when your months in India approach the limits. At 6 months or more it says the result is not reliable, because status, taxes and the treaty tie-breaker can all change the answer.

Your Dutch tax residency is a separate question. The Netherlands generally looks at where your life is centred, such as your home and family, rather than at a day count. Confirm your own position with an adviser.

We have not yet checked the official text. The day counts above come from secondary sources and our reading of the rules, not from the official Income-tax Act text. Details such as how the 120-day test applies, the deemed residency rule and the 2025 Act renumbering need confirmation by an Indian tax adviser. Do not rely on this page for a residency decision.

Frequently asked

How many days can an NRI stay in India?

Under 182 days in the financial year is the general limit. A stricter limit applies in some cases for citizens with higher Indian income. Get advice for your own case.

What is RNOR?

Resident but not ordinarily resident: a temporary status between NRI and fully resident, where India taxes only Indian income.
